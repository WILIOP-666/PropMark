## How to use this calculator
#### When to use this
You're wondering how much you can borrow before you start looking at properties. This gives you a realistic price range to search within.
#### Steps
  1. Enter your **gross annual income** (before tax).
  2. Add your **partner's income** if you're buying together — leave as $0 if buying solo.
  3. Enter your **monthly living expenses** — food, utilities, transport, subscriptions, etc.
  4. Enter any **existing debt repayments** — car loans, credit card minimums, HECS/HELP, personal loans.
  5. Enter the **deposit** you've saved so far.
  6. Adjust the **interest rate** and **loan term** if needed.


#### Understanding your results
  * **Estimated Borrowing Power** — the maximum loan amount a bank is likely to approve based on your income and expenses.
  * **Max Property Price** — your borrowing power plus your deposit. This is the top end of your budget.
  * **The +3% buffer** — banks don't test you at today's rate. They add roughly 3% on top to make sure you can still afford repayments if rates go up.
  * **Debt-to-Income Ratio** — how much of your income goes to debt. Green is healthy, amber is stretched, red means lenders may be cautious.


#### Tips
  * Pay off credit cards before applying — banks count the **full credit limit** as a liability, even if you don't use it.
  * This is an estimate — talk to a mortgage broker for an exact figure tailored to your situation.
