## Building a property intelligence layer is the hard part
Every proptech founder, AI builder, and platform developer hits the same wall. The product idea is clear. The tech stack is ready. Then you need property data.
Government sources are fragmented across states. Valuations need hedonic models calibrated against millions of sales. Demographics need ABS ingestion pipelines. Risk data sits in flood maps, bushfire overlays, and heritage registers that each use different formats. CMA requires matching on 30+ property features and adjusting for time.
Building this from scratch takes years and a dedicated data team. Or you call one API.
What Luke tells API customers:
"The upside is that you don't need to build a property database. You don't need to scrape a lot of sources, and you don't even need to do a property search. Massive saving of dev time. Rather than having to become a database expert and a data cleaning expert and a real estate expert, you just call our API, and off you go."

## Why not build it yourself?
With AI tools, everyone thinks they can build their own Microburbs. Many try. Here is what they find.
Accurate valuations
Hedonic models need millions of calibrated sales, adjusted for time, location, and 30+ property features. A naive median is dangerously wrong.
Comparative Market Analysis
Matching properties on bedrooms and bathrooms is not enough. Real CMA requires 30+ features, verified distances, risk overlays, and time-adjusted sale prices. We update CMA data daily.
Growth forecasts
Our forecasts are backtested over 15 years with 85% accuracy. Building a comparable model means assembling the historical dataset first.
Risk overlays
Flood, fire, heritage, erosion, public housing, noise, and easement data sits in different state government systems, each with different formats and update cycles.
Keeping it current
We update property data and CMA daily, suburb metrics weekly. Monthly data can cost a buyer $60,000 on a $700,000 property in a fast-moving market.
