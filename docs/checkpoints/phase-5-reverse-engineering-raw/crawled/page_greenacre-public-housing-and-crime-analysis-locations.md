# 29.5% Public Housing on Highview Avenue: Crime and Public Housing in Greenacre Analysis
![Luke Metcalfe, Data Scientist and Founder, Microburbs.](https://www.microburbs.com.au/cdn/blog-media/507f6c07777f-66613a35309b351d853c0b62_Luke-20smile.jpeg)

Greenacre, a suburb in Sydney's southwest, presents an interesting dynamic when it comes to public housing and safety. The pocket surrounding Highview Avenue has the highest concentration of public housing at 29.5%, making it a focal point for those seeking affordable homes. However, as with all areas, public housing varies greatly, with some pockets housing families, and others being more oriented towards single individuals or elderly residents.

#### Key Insights:
  * **Public Housing and Crime**
    * Pockets with significant public housing concentration, such as around Highview Avenue (29.5%) and other areas like the southern sections near Potts Hill (14.9%), create a perception of reduced safety. However, public housing does not necessarily equate to higher crime rates, and it's important to recognize that the nature of residents can vary. Some pockets may be more family-oriented, while others might attract younger residents or those in need of social support.
    * The suburb experiences some degree of crime, as noted by locals. Incidents of youth-related disturbances, drug-related issues, and street racing have been reported, particularly in the evenings. Areas around Chullora and closer to Roberts Road may feel less safe, especially after dark, with locals advising caution during those hours.
  * **Crime Statistics & Community Insights**
    * Greenacre has been known for occasional flare-ups of antisocial behavior, with concerns about safety, particularly in pockets where public housing is more concentrated. However, despite this, many locals report feeling safe during the day. Families are often seen in parks and open spaces, and the community is diverse, welcoming, and close-knit.
    * Greenacre has witnessed a few high-profile incidents, particularly related to property damage and drive-by shootings. In fact, Greenacre has been ranked as one of the top suburbs in Sydney for drive-by shootings in the past. While these incidents are alarming, they are not an everyday occurrence and are often concentrated in specific pockets rather than across the whole suburb.
  * **Safety of Surrounding Areas**
    * The northern parts of Greenacre, around Liverpool Road, are known for being quieter and generally safer. These areas, with lower concentrations of public housing (around 5.7%), are more family-friendly, and locals describe them as peaceful.
    * Bankstown to the south, which borders Greenacre, has higher crime rates and larger public housing estates. However, this area is undergoing significant redevelopment, and property values have been rising, particularly in pockets that are seeing investment in infrastructure.


#### Location of Public Housing and Impact on Property Values:
  * **Public Housing Distribution**
    * The public housing in Greenacre is pocketed, with concentrations around streets like Highview Avenue, Potts Hill, and some smaller areas near Mount Lewis. These pockets represent opportunities for first-time homebuyers or investors looking for affordable options. It’s important to note that concentrations of public housing are not spread across the entire suburb and tend to be localized.
  * **Impact on Capital Growth**
    * Areas with higher concentrations of public housing may offer more affordable entry points for buyers. However, these areas could experience slower growth in property values due to their association with crime or social challenges. On the other hand, the continued development and proximity to key transport routes like the M4 and M5 motorways present strong potential for capital growth in surrounding areas.
    * Greenacre is seeing an increase in demand for homes, particularly as younger families move in. This is pushing up property values, particularly in pockets that are further from public housing concentrations.


#### Crime and Safety in Greenacre:
  * Greenacre is a suburb of contrasts when it comes to safety. While some locals express concerns over youth disturbances, street racing, and drug-related incidents, others feel safe and comfortable, particularly during the day. It is important to note that many of these issues are concentrated in specific pockets, particularly those with higher levels of public housing.
  * Families and retirees are often drawn to the quieter, more residential pockets of Greenacre, where crime rates are lower, and the community vibe is stronger. The streets near schools, parks, and family homes tend to feel safer, even in the evening.


#### Is Greenacre a Safe Place to Live?
Greenacre offers a mix of opportunities for homebuyers and investors. While it has some pockets with elevated crime concerns, these tend to be isolated and do not represent the entirety of the suburb. The proximity to major highways, diverse community, and ongoing redevelopment are positives for the area. Families looking for quieter streets with a strong community feel may want to focus on areas away from the higher concentrations of public housing, such as the northern parts or pockets near Strathfield and Lakemba.
**Tips for Buyers:**
  * If you're looking to invest in Greenacre, focus on pockets that are seeing more growth and development, such as areas near schools and transport links.
  * Public housing pockets may offer affordable options, but be mindful of their potential impact on long-term capital growth.
  * For those concerned about safety, it may be better to avoid areas closer to the southern parts bordering Bankstown, which have higher crime rates.


In conclusion, Greenacre is a suburb with plenty of potential. While safety concerns exist in certain areas, the overall sense of community, diversity, and growing property demand make it an attractive option for both investors and families looking to settle.
