# Australian Property Market Convenience Score: A Validated Suburb-Level Indicator
Luke Metcalfe, Microburbs Research
June 2026
## 1. Abstract
This whitepaper documents the convenience dimension of the Microburbs nine-dimension livability score for Australian residential suburbs. Each Australian residential microburb (street-block of approximately 30 to 60 dwellings) is scored on this dimension on a 0 to 10 scale, and the suburb-level aggregate is validated against actual market outcomes for 4,127 suburbs.
Convenience correlates with house prices at +0.39, weekly rents at +0.26, and gross rental yields at -0.34 (the second-largest negative yield correlation of any dimension). It also has a notable NEGATIVE correlation with 5-year growth (-0.20), suggesting convenient suburbs are largely priced in.

## 2. What the convenience dimension measures
The convenience dimension is built from the following inputs: _distance to bus stops, train stations, supermarkets, medical facilities, restaurants, fast food outlets, road network density, and walkability_.
Convenience captures the practical accessibility of daily life: how much can be accomplished without a car. Convenient suburbs trade suburban quiet for amenity proximity, and they tend to be paid for in two ways — higher prices and lower yields.

## 5. Interpretation
The convenience dimension is one input among nine. By itself it predicts current price level and rent level for Australian suburbs at the strengths shown in Section 3. It does not, on its own, predict capital growth direction over the medium term — that question requires a separate temporal analysis that this score does not attempt.
The right use of the convenience dimension for an investor is to understand which feature of a suburb is driving its price position. For a buyer-occupier, it can be combined with other dimensions to find suburbs that match specific priorities (e.g. high convenience + high tranquility + high family).
## 6. Limitations
  * The score is cross-sectional and does not capture trajectory.
  * Validation is at suburb level (n=4,127). Microburb-level validation is not directly tested.
  * Correlations are observational and do not imply causation.
  * The convenience dimension shares some underlying inputs with other dimensions (notably affluence, safety, and economic). Joint use of multiple dimensions does not double-count cleanly.
