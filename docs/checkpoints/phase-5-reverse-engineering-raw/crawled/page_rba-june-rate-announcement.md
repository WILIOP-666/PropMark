# Breaking News: RBA Announces Another Rate Hike, Sending Shockwaves through the Australian Economy
![Luke Metcalfe](https://www.microburbs.com.au/cdn/blog-media/8afa250d100e-66a04ac3bc627c5d1992a6be_Annotation-202020-04-08-20145202.jpg)
Luke MetcalfeJune 6, 2023
In breaking news, the Reserve Bank of Australia (RBA) has just announced another rate hike for June, [increasing the cash rate by 25 basis points to 4.10%](https://www.rba.gov.au/media-releases/2023/mr-23-13.html). The decision comes amidst concerns over high inflation and an overheating housing market, prompting the central bank to take action to rein in borrowing and spending.
This move comes as no surprise to analysts who have been closely watching the RBA's policy decisions. The central bank has been signaling for some time that it may need to take action to cool down the economy, which has been running hot in recent months. With unemployment at a [low rate of 3.7%](https://www.rba.gov.au/media-releases/2023/mr-23-13.html) and [award wages set to rise 5.75%](https://www.fwc.gov.au/documents/resources/annual-wage-review-2022-23-decision-announcement.pdf) from July 1st, the board remains alert to the risks of ongoing high inflation.
In a [statement](https://www.rba.gov.au/media-releases/2023/mr-23-13.html), RBA Governor Philip Lowe said that the rate hike "is to provide greater confidence that inflation will return to target within a reasonable timeframe". He noted that further rate hikes may be required in the coming months to ensure that inflation returns to target.
The decision has been met with mixed reactions, with some economists hailing it as a necessary step to curb inflation, while others warn of the potential negative impact on households and businesses.
The rate hike will have a significant impact on mortgage holders, with many borrowers set to face higher monthly repayments. The average Australian borrower will now be paying an [additional $15,000 per year in repayments](https://www.9news.com.au/finance/australia-interest-rates-reserve-bank-of-australia-june-decision-2023/dfbe8e6b-970a-4167-ab71-ba95ba600ee8) compared to 12 months ago.
Overall, the RBA's decision to raise interest rates for the second time in as many months is a clear indication of the central bank's determination to keep inflation under control. While the move is likely to have some short-term negative impacts on households and businesses, it is hoped that it will ultimately help to ensure a more stable and sustainable economic future for Australia.
