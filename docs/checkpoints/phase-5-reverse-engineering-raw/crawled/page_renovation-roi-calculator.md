## Assumptions
  * •Capital growth is applied equally to both the renovated and non-renovated property values at the same annual rate.
  * •The alternative investment return is compounded annually on the full renovation cost.
  * •This does not factor in rental yield improvements from renovation, tax implications (depreciation, CGT), or financing costs.
  * •The “expected value after renovation” is your most critical input — get it appraised by a local agent before committing.


This is a simplified estimate only. Renovation outcomes depend on scope, quality, location, and market conditions — consult a property professional for advice specific to your circumstances.
## How to use this calculator
### When to use this
You're planning a renovation and want to know if it's worth it financially — or if you'd be better off investing the cash elsewhere.
### Steps
  1. Enter your property's current market value.
  2. Enter the total renovation cost you're considering.
  3. Estimate the expected property value after renovation — ask a local agent for a realistic post-reno appraisal.
  4. Select how long you plan to hold the property after renovating.
  5. Review the year-by-year comparison to see when (or if) the renovation pays off versus investing the cash.


### Tips
  * **Kitchens and bathrooms** typically add the most value relative to their cost — focus on these for maximum ROI.
  * **Overcapitalisation risk** — don't spend more than the suburb ceiling allows. Your renovated property can't exceed what the market will pay in that location.
  * **Consider opportunity cost** — the renovation money could be earning returns elsewhere in shares, ETFs, or another property deposit.
  * **Get 3 quotes and add 20% contingency** to your budget. Builder costs can vary 30–50% for the same scope and surprises are common in reno projects.
