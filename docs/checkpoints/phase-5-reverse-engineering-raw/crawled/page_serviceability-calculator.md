## How to use this calculator
#### When to use this
You want a detailed estimate of how much you can borrow, factoring in all your financial commitments — not just income and expenses, but credit cards, HECS, investment loans, and rental income too.
#### Steps
  1. Enter your **gross annual income** and your partner's if buying together.
  2. Set the **number of dependants** — this affects the HEM benchmark.
  3. Enter your **monthly living expenses** , or leave at 0 to use the HEM benchmark (banks use whichever is higher).
  4. Enter all **existing debts** — home loans, investment loans, car loans, personal loans.
  5. Enter your **credit card total limits** (not balances) and any **HECS/HELP debt**.
  6. Add **rental income** from any existing investment properties.
  7. Adjust the **interest rate** and **loan term** if needed.


#### Understanding your results
  * **Max Borrowing Amount** — the maximum loan a bank is likely to approve based on your full financial picture.
  * **Max Property Price** — assumes a 20% deposit (80% LVR). Your borrowing amount divided by 0.8.
  * **Assessment Rate** — banks add a 3% buffer above the actual rate to stress-test your repayments.
  * **DTI Ratio** — total annual debt divided by gross income. Green (<6x) is healthy, amber (6–8x) is stretched, red (>8x) may trigger lender caution.
  * **Monthly Breakdown** — shows exactly how your income is allocated: tax, expenses, commitments, and what's left for a mortgage.


#### Tips
  * Close unused credit cards — banks count the **full limit** even with $0 balance. A $20k limit you never use still reduces your borrowing power.
  * Paying off **HECS before applying** can significantly increase borrowing power, especially if your income triggers a high repayment rate.
  * Banks use **80% of rental income** , so existing investment properties help but not dollar-for-dollar.
  * This is an estimate — talk to a mortgage broker for an exact figure tailored to your lender's specific policies.
