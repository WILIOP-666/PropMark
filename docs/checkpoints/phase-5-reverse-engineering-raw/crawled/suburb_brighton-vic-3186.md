Is Brighton family-friendly?Age, family structure and household size describe the community families would be joining.+
15.6% of Brighton's residents were children under 15 in the 2021 Census. That is somewhat below the Greater Melbourne average.
6,406 family households lived in Brighton; 45.5% were couple families and 12.1% were one-parent families.
32.8% of local families included children under 15. That is well below the Greater Melbourne average.
These are suburb-wide household figures, not school or childcare ratings.
What is the age profile of Brighton?The balance between younger adults, families and older residents changes how a suburb feels day to day.+
Brighton had 23,252 residents at the 2021 Census.
18.4% of residents were aged 25 to 44.
39.3% were 55 or older. That is well above the Greater Melbourne average.
Age shares use the suburb’s total Census population.
What kind of homes are in Brighton?The dwelling mix shows whether the suburb is mostly detached homes, townhouses or apartments.+
Separate houses made up 57.2% of occupied private dwellings in Brighton.
Townhouses and other attached homes represented 22.4% of occupied private dwellings.
Flats and apartments accounted for 19.8% of occupied private dwellings.
All three shares use occupied private dwellings as the denominator.
Do people own or rent in Brighton?Tenure shows how many households own outright, have a mortgage or rent.+
46.5% of households with a stated tenure owned their home outright. That is well above the Greater Melbourne average.
29.0% were buying their home with a mortgage.
24.5% rented their home. That is well below the Greater Melbourne average.
Tenure shares include households that stated one of these three arrangements.
How large are households in Brighton?Household-size data shows the mix behind the suburb-wide average.+
The average household in Brighton had 2.4 people.
One-person households made up 27.8% of occupied homes. That is somewhat above the Greater Melbourne average.
Two-person households represented 34.7%, while 23.1% had four or more usual residents.
Household size counts usual residents in occupied homes.
How do people get to work in Brighton?Census-day travel shows the balance between home, road, public transport and active travel.+
44.9% of workers worked from home on Census day. That is well above the Greater Melbourne average.
33.5% drove a car as their only travel method. That is well below the Greater Melbourne average.
3.1% used only a train, tram or bus, while 3.4% walked or cycled.
Travel figures are a 2021 Census-day snapshot.
How many cars do households have in Brighton?Vehicle availability helps describe the routines local households can support.+
5.0% of occupied households had no motor vehicle available. That is somewhat below the Greater Melbourne average.
One-car households formed 39.3% of the total.
55.7% of households had two or more vehicles.
Vehicle counts refer to occupied households, not individual residents.
Who's moving to Brighton, and who stays?Address history shows how settled or recently mobile residents were at the Census date.+
58.6% were at the same address five years earlier, while 41.4% reported a different address.
27.4% of residents were born outside Australia. That is well below the Greater Melbourne average.
Mobility figures cover residents who answered the relevant Census question.
What languages are spoken in Brighton?Language at home gives a direct view of the community’s everyday linguistic mix.+
80.5% of residents used only English at home.
15.7% used a language other than English at home.
4.0% spoke Mandarin at home, 0.6% spoke Cantonese and 0.3% spoke Hindi.
Home language is separate from English proficiency.
Where were Brighton residents born?Birthplace adds another layer to the local story, separate from ancestry and language.+
27.4% of Brighton's population was born outside Australia, while roughly 72.6% was born in Australia.
3.5% of residents were born in China.

People may nominate more than one ancestry, so these are shares of responses.
What work do people do in Brighton?Occupation describes a person’s role; industry describes the sector employing them.+

Managers accounted for 26.0% of employed residents, while clerical and administrative workers made up 11.2%.
Health care and social assistance employed 12.5% of workers, and education and training employed 8.2%.
Occupation and industry figures describe employed residents of the suburb.
How active is the workforce in Brighton?Participation, employment pattern and unemployment describe the resident labour force.+

33.3% of residents aged 15 and over worked full time, while 20.5% worked part time.
Unemployment was 3.7%, compared with 5.3% across Greater Melbourne.
Labour-force figures come from the 2021 Census.

6.3% reported a long-term mental health condition, including depression or anxiety.
6.9% reported asthma, 7.9% arthritis and 2.7% diabetes excluding gestational diabetes.
These are self-reported suburb-wide Census figures, not address-level health risks.
How educated is Brighton?Qualifications describe the study pathways represented among local residents.+

Postgraduate degrees accounted for 17.3% of qualification responses.
Diploma-level qualifications represented 13.9%, while certificate-level qualifications represented 10.7%.
Qualification categories are reported separately and are not school ratings.
