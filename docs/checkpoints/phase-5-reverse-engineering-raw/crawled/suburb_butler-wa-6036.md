## Questions people ask about Butler
Open any question for a plain-English reading of the public 2021 Census for Butler WA 6036.
Is Butler family-friendly?Age, family structure and household size describe the community families would be joining.+
23.8% of Butler's residents were children under 15 in the 2021 Census. That is somewhat above the Greater Perth average.
3,628 family households lived in Butler; 47.9% were couple families and 23.8% were one-parent families.
48.9% of local families included children under 15. That is well above the Greater Perth average.
These are suburb-wide household figures, not school or childcare ratings.
What is the age profile of Butler?The balance between younger adults, families and older residents changes how a suburb feels day to day.+
Butler had 13,473 residents at the 2021 Census.
28.9% of residents were aged 25 to 44.
17.0% were 55 or older. That is well below the Greater Perth average.
Age shares use the suburb’s total Census population.
What kind of homes are in Butler?The dwelling mix shows whether the suburb is mostly detached homes, townhouses or apartments.+
Separate houses made up 96.0% of occupied private dwellings in Butler.
Townhouses and other attached homes represented 3.0% of occupied private dwellings.
Flats and apartments accounted for 0.9% of occupied private dwellings.
All three shares use occupied private dwellings as the denominator.
Do people own or rent in Butler?Tenure shows how many households own outright, have a mortgage or rent.+
12.7% of households with a stated tenure owned their home outright. That is about half the Greater Perth rate.
52.1% were buying their home with a mortgage.
35.2% rented their home. That is well above the Greater Perth average.
Tenure shares include households that stated one of these three arrangements.
How large are households in Butler?Household-size data shows the mix behind the suburb-wide average.+
The average household in Butler had 2.8 people.
One-person households made up 21.4% of occupied homes. That is somewhat below the Greater Perth average.
Two-person households represented 28.5%, while 31.6% had four or more usual residents.
Household size counts usual residents in occupied homes.
How do people get to work in Butler?Census-day travel shows the balance between home, road, public transport and active travel.+
5.6% of workers worked from home on Census day. That is close to the Greater Perth average.
59.1% drove a car as their only travel method. That is somewhat below the Greater Perth average.
6.7% used only a train, tram or bus, while 1.2% walked or cycled.
Travel figures are a 2021 Census-day snapshot.
How many cars do households have in Butler?Vehicle availability helps describe the routines local households can support.+
4.7% of occupied households had no motor vehicle available. That is almost exactly typical for Greater Perth.
One-car households formed 39.9% of the total.
55.6% of households had two or more vehicles.
Vehicle counts refer to occupied households, not individual residents.
Who's moving to Butler, and who stays?Address history shows how settled or recently mobile residents were at the Census date.+
57.5% were at the same address five years earlier, while 42.5% reported a different address.
43.1% of residents were born outside Australia. That is well above the Greater Perth average.
Mobility figures cover residents who answered the relevant Census question.
What languages are spoken in Butler?Language at home gives a direct view of the community’s everyday linguistic mix.+
80.6% of residents used only English at home.
14.5% used a language other than English at home.
0.5% spoke Mandarin at home, 0.2% spoke Cantonese and 0.3% spoke Hindi.
Home language is separate from English proficiency.
Where were Butler residents born?Birthplace adds another layer to the local story, separate from ancestry and language.+
43.1% of Butler's population was born outside Australia, while roughly 56.9% was born in Australia.
0.3% of residents were born in China.
1.2% were born in India, while Australian citizenship across the whole suburb stood at 80.0%.
Birthplace, citizenship and ancestry describe different parts of identity.
What ancestries shape Butler?Ancestry responses show how residents describe family and cultural background.+
English ancestry accounted for 46.4% of ancestry responses in Butler.
Australian ancestry made up 26.4% of responses.
Chinese ancestry represented 1.5% of responses.
People may nominate more than one ancestry, so these are shares of responses.
What work do people do in Butler?Occupation describes a person’s role; industry describes the sector employing them.+
14.2% of employed residents worked in professional occupations. That is well below the Greater Perth average.
Managers accounted for 8.6% of employed residents, while clerical and administrative workers made up 12.3%.
Health care and social assistance employed 14.6% of workers, and education and training employed 7.6%.
Occupation and industry figures describe employed residents of the suburb.
How active is the workforce in Butler?Participation, employment pattern and unemployment describe the resident labour force.+
The labour-force participation rate was 69.5%, compared with 65.2% across Greater Perth.
38.2% of residents aged 15 and over worked full time, while 22.4% worked part time.
Unemployment was 6.9%, compared with 5.3% across Greater Perth.
Labour-force figures come from the 2021 Census.
What does the health profile of Butler show?Selected long-term health conditions help describe community service needs.+
62.7% reported no selected long-term health condition.
10.1% reported a long-term mental health condition, including depression or anxiety.
8.6% reported asthma, 6.1% arthritis and 3.6% diabetes excluding gestational diabetes.
These are self-reported suburb-wide Census figures, not address-level health risks.
How educated is Butler?Qualifications describe the study pathways represented among local residents.+
17.8% of people in the qualifications table held a bachelor degree. That is well below the Greater Perth average.
Postgraduate degrees accounted for 4.1% of qualification responses.
Diploma-level qualifications represented 17.2%, while certificate-level qualifications represented 45.3%.
Qualification categories are reported separately and are not school ratings.
What do incomes and housing costs look like in Butler?Census income and household payments give historical context for local living costs.+
Median household income was $1,710 per week in the 2021 Census.
The median monthly mortgage repayment reported by local households was $1,733.
Occupied renting households reported a median rent of $340 per week.
These household figures are from 2021; current sale and asking-rent medians appear in the market section.
How does Butler compare with Greater Perth?A few clear differences place the suburb in its wider metropolitan context.+

Renting accounted for 35.2% in Butler, against 27.5% across Greater Perth.
