Is Byford family-friendly?Age, family structure and household size describe the community families would be joining.+
28.9% of Byford's residents were children under 15 in the 2021 Census. That is well above the Greater Perth average.
5,098 family households lived in Byford; 55.1% were couple families and 15.8% were one-parent families.
55.7% of local families included children under 15. That is well above the Greater Perth average.
These are suburb-wide household figures, not school or childcare ratings.
What is the age profile of Byford?The balance between younger adults, families and older residents changes how a suburb feels day to day.+
Byford had 18,878 residents at the 2021 Census.
35.0% of residents were aged 25 to 44.
14.9% were 55 or older. That is about half the Greater Perth rate.
Age shares use the suburb’s total Census population.
What kind of homes are in Byford?The dwelling mix shows whether the suburb is mostly detached homes, townhouses or apartments.+
Separate houses made up 98.7% of occupied private dwellings in Byford.
Townhouses and other attached homes represented 1.1% of occupied private dwellings.
Flats and apartments accounted for 0.1% of occupied private dwellings.
All three shares use occupied private dwellings as the denominator.
Do people own or rent in Byford?Tenure shows how many households own outright, have a mortgage or rent.+
14.2% of households with a stated tenure owned their home outright. That is about half the Greater Perth rate.
70.1% were buying their home with a mortgage.
15.6% rented their home. That is well below the Greater Perth average.
Tenure shares include households that stated one of these three arrangements.
How large are households in Byford?Household-size data shows the mix behind the suburb-wide average.+
The average household in Byford had 3.0 people.
One-person households made up 15.1% of occupied homes. That is well below the Greater Perth average.
Two-person households represented 28.1%, while 38.0% had four or more usual residents.
Household size counts usual residents in occupied homes.
How do people get to work in Byford?Census-day travel shows the balance between home, road, public transport and active travel.+
5.0% of workers worked from home on Census day. That is somewhat below the Greater Perth average.
68.1% drove a car as their only travel method. That is well above the Greater Perth average.
2.4% used only a train, tram or bus, while 1.1% walked or cycled.
Travel figures are a 2021 Census-day snapshot.
How many cars do households have in Byford?Vehicle availability helps describe the routines local households can support.+
1.4% of occupied households had no motor vehicle available. That is about half the Greater Perth rate.
One-car households formed 26.4% of the total.
72.3% of households had two or more vehicles.
Vehicle counts refer to occupied households, not individual residents.
Who's moving to Byford, and who stays?Address history shows how settled or recently mobile residents were at the Census date.+
57.7% were at the same address five years earlier, while 42.3% reported a different address.
27.7% of residents were born outside Australia. That is well below the Greater Perth average.
Mobility figures cover residents who answered the relevant Census question.
What languages are spoken in Byford?Language at home gives a direct view of the community’s everyday linguistic mix.+
80.9% of residents used only English at home.
14.9% used a language other than English at home.
0.5% spoke Mandarin at home, 0.2% spoke Cantonese and 0.4% spoke Hindi.
Home language is separate from English proficiency.
Where were Byford residents born?Birthplace adds another layer to the local story, separate from ancestry and language.+
27.7% of Byford's population was born outside Australia, while roughly 72.3% was born in Australia.
0.2% of residents were born in China.
3.5% were born in India, while Australian citizenship across the whole suburb stood at 85.4%.

What work do people do in Byford?Occupation describes a person’s role; industry describes the sector employing them.+
12.4% of employed residents worked in professional occupations. That is about half the Greater Perth rate.
Managers accounted for 8.7% of employed residents, while clerical and administrative workers made up 13.5%.
Health care and social assistance employed 13.1% of workers, and education and training employed 6.8%.
Occupation and industry figures describe employed residents of the suburb.
How active is the workforce in Byford?Participation, employment pattern and unemployment describe the resident labour force.+

44.0% of residents aged 15 and over worked full time, while 20.9% worked part time.
Unemployment was 4.3%, compared with 5.3% across Greater Perth.
Labour-force figures come from the 2021 Census.

8.8% reported a long-term mental health condition, including depression or anxiety.
8.4% reported asthma, 5.2% arthritis and 3.8% diabetes excluding gestational diabetes.
These are self-reported suburb-wide Census figures, not address-level health risks.
How educated is Byford?Qualifications describe the study pathways represented among local residents.+

Postgraduate degrees accounted for 3.6% of qualification responses.
Diploma-level qualifications represented 17.0%, while certificate-level qualifications represented 50.1%.
Qualification categories are reported separately and are not school ratings.
