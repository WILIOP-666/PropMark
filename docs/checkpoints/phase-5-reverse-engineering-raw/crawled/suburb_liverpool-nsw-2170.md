The median house price in Liverpool, NSW is currently $1,200,000, based on the latest 12 months of sales. Liverpool has a 12-month growth rate of 2.6% for houses and 4.8% for units.
Two-bedroom houses have a median sale price of $585,000, Three-bedroom houses have a median sale price of $1,150,000, Four-bedroom houses have a median sale price of $1,240,000, Larger houses have a median sale price of $1,200,000 in Liverpool.
If you're looking to lease, the median rent for houses in Liverpool is $675 per week. The median house rent has increased 8.0% over the last 12 months.
The median price of apartments and units for sale in Liverpool is $535,000, which has increased 4.8% since the same time last year. One-bedroom apartments and units have a median sale price of $421,000, Two-bedroom apartments and units have a median sale price of $530,000, Three-bedroom apartments and units have a median sale price of $692,000, Four-bedroom apartments and units have a median sale price of $971,000 in Liverpool. The median rent for units and apartments in Liverpool is $550 per week and has increased 4.8% over the last 12 months.

## Questions people ask about Liverpool
Open any question for a plain-English reading of the public 2021 Census for Liverpool NSW 2170.
Is Liverpool family-friendly?Age, family structure and household size describe the community families would be joining.+
19.5% of Liverpool's residents were children under 15 in the 2021 Census. That is close to the Greater Sydney average.
7,647 family households lived in Liverpool; 45.2% were couple families and 23.2% were one-parent families.
42.6% of local families included children under 15. That is somewhat above the Greater Sydney average.
These are suburb-wide household figures, not school or childcare ratings.
What is the age profile of Liverpool?The balance between younger adults, families and older residents changes how a suburb feels day to day.+
Liverpool had 31,078 residents at the 2021 Census.
35.1% of residents were aged 25 to 44.
21.9% were 55 or older. That is somewhat below the Greater Sydney average.
Age shares use the suburb’s total Census population.
What kind of homes are in Liverpool?The dwelling mix shows whether the suburb is mostly detached homes, townhouses or apartments.+
Separate houses made up 24.3% of occupied private dwellings in Liverpool.
Townhouses and other attached homes represented 10.1% of occupied private dwellings.
Flats and apartments accounted for 65.0% of occupied private dwellings.
All three shares use occupied private dwellings as the denominator.
Do people own or rent in Liverpool?Tenure shows how many households own outright, have a mortgage or rent.+
15.7% of households with a stated tenure owned their home outright. That is about half the Greater Sydney rate.
20.8% were buying their home with a mortgage.
63.5% rented their home. That is well above the Greater Sydney average.
Tenure shares include households that stated one of these three arrangements.
How large are households in Liverpool?Household-size data shows the mix behind the suburb-wide average.+
The average household in Liverpool had 2.6 people.
One-person households made up 28.9% of occupied homes. That is somewhat above the Greater Sydney average.
Two-person households represented 28.6%, while 24.9% had four or more usual residents.
Household size counts usual residents in occupied homes.
How do people get to work in Liverpool?Census-day travel shows the balance between home, road, public transport and active travel.+
22.7% of workers worked from home on Census day. That is well below the Greater Sydney average.
43.2% drove a car as their only travel method. That is well above the Greater Sydney average.
5.0% used only a train, tram or bus, while 4.2% walked or cycled.
Travel figures are a 2021 Census-day snapshot.
How many cars do households have in Liverpool?Vehicle availability helps describe the routines local households can support.+
17.4% of occupied households had no motor vehicle available. That is well above the Greater Sydney average.
One-car households formed 50.2% of the total.
32.5% of households had two or more vehicles.
Vehicle counts refer to occupied households, not individual residents.
Who's moving to Liverpool, and who stays?Address history shows how settled or recently mobile residents were at the Census date.+
46.4% were at the same address five years earlier, while 53.6% reported a different address.
56.6% of residents were born outside Australia. That is well above the Greater Sydney average.
Mobility figures cover residents who answered the relevant Census question.
What languages are spoken in Liverpool?Language at home gives a direct view of the community’s everyday linguistic mix.+
24.6% of residents used only English at home.
64.9% used a language other than English at home.
1.7% spoke Mandarin at home, 1.2% spoke Cantonese and 3.2% spoke Hindi.
Home language is separate from English proficiency.
Where were Liverpool residents born?Birthplace adds another layer to the local story, separate from ancestry and language.+
56.6% of Liverpool's population was born outside Australia, while roughly 43.4% was born in Australia.
1.7% of residents were born in China.
4.9% were born in India, while Australian citizenship across the whole suburb stood at 68.7%.
Birthplace, citizenship and ancestry describe different parts of identity.
What ancestries shape Liverpool?Ancestry responses show how residents describe family and cultural background.+
English ancestry accounted for 8.3% of ancestry responses in Liverpool.
Australian ancestry made up 9.4% of responses.
Chinese ancestry represented 4.7% of responses.
People may nominate more than one ancestry, so these are shares of responses.
What work do people do in Liverpool?Occupation describes a person’s role; industry describes the sector employing them.+
19.7% of employed residents worked in professional occupations. That is well below the Greater Sydney average.
Managers accounted for 7.8% of employed residents, while clerical and administrative workers made up 13.6%.
Health care and social assistance employed 18.7% of workers, and education and training employed 5.4%.
Occupation and industry figures describe employed residents of the suburb.
How active is the workforce in Liverpool?Participation, employment pattern and unemployment describe the resident labour force.+
The labour-force participation rate was 44.9%, compared with 60.0% across Greater Sydney.
21.9% of residents aged 15 and over worked full time, while 11.6% worked part time.
Unemployment was 10.2%, compared with 5.1% across Greater Sydney.
Labour-force figures come from the 2021 Census.
What does the health profile of Liverpool show?Selected long-term health conditions help describe community service needs.+
63.1% reported no selected long-term health condition.
6.0% reported a long-term mental health condition, including depression or anxiety.
5.6% reported asthma, 5.8% arthritis and 5.7% diabetes excluding gestational diabetes.
These are self-reported suburb-wide Census figures, not address-level health risks.
How educated is Liverpool?Qualifications describe the study pathways represented among local residents.+
24.3% of people in the qualifications table held a bachelor degree. That is well below the Greater Sydney average.
Postgraduate degrees accounted for 9.7% of qualification responses.
Diploma-level qualifications represented 16.6%, while certificate-level qualifications represented 24.2%.
Qualification categories are reported separately and are not school ratings.
What do incomes and housing costs look like in Liverpool?Census income and household payments give historical context for local living costs.+
Median household income was $1,303 per week in the 2021 Census.
The median monthly mortgage repayment reported by local households was $1,733.
Occupied renting households reported a median rent of $370 per week.
These household figures are from 2021; current sale and asking-rent medians appear in the market section.
How does Liverpool compare with Greater Sydney?A few clear differences place the suburb in its wider metropolitan context.+
Working from home was 22.7% in Liverpool, compared with 38.9% across Greater Sydney.
Outright ownership was 15.7% locally and 28.6% across Greater Sydney. That is about half the Greater Sydney rate.
Renting accounted for 63.5% in Liverpool, against 37.1% across Greater Sydney.
Capital-region comparisons use the same Census definitions and year.

## A buyer’s quick guide to Liverpool
### Is Liverpool expensive?
The median house price is $1,200,000, while units and apartments sit at $535,000. Bedroom count, land and condition still create a wide range around those suburb medians.
### Is Liverpool good for families?
The Census counted 7,647 local families, and 19.5% of residents were under 15. That describes the community; school quality, travel times and the right fit for a particular family need separate research.
### What can I get for $1.5m in Liverpool?
4-bedroom houses have a median around $1,240,000, the closest bedroom benchmark we hold to a $1.5m budget. Individual homes can differ substantially with block size, renovation and location.
Medians describe recorded market activity across the suburb; they are historical context, not a valuation or forecast for an individual home.
