## Questions people ask about Perth
Open any question for a plain-English reading of the public 2021 Census for Perth WA 6000.
Is Perth family-friendly?Age, family structure and household size describe the community families would be joining.+
6.9% of Perth's residents were children under 15 in the 2021 Census. That is about half the Greater Perth rate.
2,727 family households lived in Perth; 21.9% were couple families and 8.9% were one-parent families.
22.3% of local families included children under 15. That is well below the Greater Perth average.
These are suburb-wide household figures, not school or childcare ratings.
What is the age profile of Perth?The balance between younger adults, families and older residents changes how a suburb feels day to day.+
Perth had 13,670 residents at the 2021 Census.
53.7% of residents were aged 25 to 44.
15.3% were 55 or older. That is well below the Greater Perth average.
Age shares use the suburb’s total Census population.
What kind of homes are in Perth?The dwelling mix shows whether the suburb is mostly detached homes, townhouses or apartments.+
Separate houses made up 8.6% of occupied private dwellings in Perth.
Townhouses and other attached homes represented 11.1% of occupied private dwellings.
Flats and apartments accounted for 79.9% of occupied private dwellings.
All three shares use occupied private dwellings as the denominator.
Do people own or rent in Perth?Tenure shows how many households own outright, have a mortgage or rent.+
12.4% of households with a stated tenure owned their home outright. That is about half the Greater Perth rate.
20.7% were buying their home with a mortgage.
66.9% rented their home. That is about twice the Greater Perth rate.
Tenure shares include households that stated one of these three arrangements.
How large are households in Perth?Household-size data shows the mix behind the suburb-wide average.+
The average household in Perth had 1.8 people.
One-person households made up 44.1% of occupied homes. That is well above the Greater Perth average.
Two-person households represented 39.7%, while 6.0% had four or more usual residents.
Household size counts usual residents in occupied homes.
How do people get to work in Perth?Census-day travel shows the balance between home, road, public transport and active travel.+
8.1% of workers worked from home on Census day. That is almost exactly typical for Greater Perth.
31.5% drove a car as their only travel method. That is about half the Greater Perth rate.
13.9% used only a train, tram or bus, while 23.3% walked or cycled.
Travel figures are a 2021 Census-day snapshot.
How many cars do households have in Perth?Vehicle availability helps describe the routines local households can support.+
23.4% of occupied households had no motor vehicle available. That is about twice the Greater Perth rate.
One-car households formed 56.6% of the total.
20.0% of households had two or more vehicles.
Vehicle counts refer to occupied households, not individual residents.
Who's moving to Perth, and who stays?Address history shows how settled or recently mobile residents were at the Census date.+
25.4% were at the same address five years earlier, while 74.6% reported a different address.
49.9% of residents were born outside Australia. That is well above the Greater Perth average.
Mobility figures cover residents who answered the relevant Census question.
What languages are spoken in Perth?Language at home gives a direct view of the community’s everyday linguistic mix.+
53.0% of residents used only English at home.
34.9% used a language other than English at home.
7.7% spoke Mandarin at home, 2.2% spoke Cantonese and 1.1% spoke Hindi.
Home language is separate from English proficiency.
Where were Perth residents born?Birthplace adds another layer to the local story, separate from ancestry and language.+
49.9% of Perth's population was born outside Australia, while roughly 50.1% was born in Australia.
5.7% of residents were born in China.
4.0% were born in India, while Australian citizenship across the whole suburb stood at 57.1%.
Birthplace, citizenship and ancestry describe different parts of identity.
What ancestries shape Perth?Ancestry responses show how residents describe family and cultural background.+
English ancestry accounted for 24.4% of ancestry responses in Perth.
Australian ancestry made up 15.4% of responses.
Chinese ancestry represented 13.3% of responses.
People may nominate more than one ancestry, so these are shares of responses.
What work do people do in Perth?Occupation describes a person’s role; industry describes the sector employing them.+
36.2% of employed residents worked in professional occupations. That is well above the Greater Perth average.
Managers accounted for 13.9% of employed residents, while clerical and administrative workers made up 10.8%.
Health care and social assistance employed 11.8% of workers, and education and training employed 5.3%.
Occupation and industry figures describe employed residents of the suburb.
How active is the workforce in Perth?Participation, employment pattern and unemployment describe the resident labour force.+
The labour-force participation rate was 69.4%, compared with 65.2% across Greater Perth.
44.3% of residents aged 15 and over worked full time, while 17.8% worked part time.
Unemployment was 5.4%, compared with 5.3% across Greater Perth.
Labour-force figures come from the 2021 Census.
What does the health profile of Perth show?Selected long-term health conditions help describe community service needs.+
64.9% reported no selected long-term health condition.
8.7% reported a long-term mental health condition, including depression or anxiety.
5.8% reported asthma, 3.1% arthritis and 2.1% diabetes excluding gestational diabetes.
These are self-reported suburb-wide Census figures, not address-level health risks.
How educated is Perth?Qualifications describe the study pathways represented among local residents.+
36.7% of people in the qualifications table held a bachelor degree. That is well above the Greater Perth average.
Postgraduate degrees accounted for 15.1% of qualification responses.
Diploma-level qualifications represented 12.3%, while certificate-level qualifications represented 14.2%.
Qualification categories are reported separately and are not school ratings.
What do incomes and housing costs look like in Perth?Census income and household payments give historical context for local living costs.+
Median household income was $1,960 per week in the 2021 Census.
The median monthly mortgage repayment reported by local households was $1,950.
Occupied renting households reported a median rent of $400 per week.
These household figures are from 2021; current sale and asking-rent medians appear in the market section.
How does Perth compare with Greater Perth?A few clear differences place the suburb in its wider metropolitan context.+
Working from home was 8.1% in Perth, compared with 7.6% across Greater Perth.
Outright ownership was 12.4% locally and 29.3% across Greater Perth. That is about half the Greater Perth rate.
Renting accounted for 66.9% in Perth, against 27.5% across Greater Perth.
Capital-region comparisons use the same Census definitions and year.

## A buyer’s quick guide to Perth (WA)
### Is Perth (WA) expensive?
The median house price is $1,290,000, while units and apartments sit at $645,000. Bedroom count, land and condition still create a wide range around those suburb medians.
### Is Perth (WA) good for families?
The Census counted 2,727 local families, and 6.9% of residents were under 15. That describes the community; school quality, travel times and the right fit for a particular family need separate research.
### What can I get for $1.5m in Perth (WA)?
4-bedroom houses have a median around $1,600,000, the closest bedroom benchmark we hold to a $1.5m budget. Individual homes can differ substantially with block size, renovation and location.
Medians describe recorded market activity across the suburb; they are historical context, not a valuation or forecast for an individual home.
