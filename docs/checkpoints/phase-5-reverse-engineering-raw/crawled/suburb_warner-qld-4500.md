## Questions people ask about Warner
Open any question for a plain-English reading of the public 2021 Census for Warner QLD 4500.
Is Warner family-friendly?Age, family structure and household size describe the community families would be joining.+
24.2% of Warner's residents were children under 15 in the 2021 Census. That is somewhat above the Greater Brisbane average.
3,576 family households lived in Warner; 53.6% were couple families and 13.7% were one-parent families.
47.0% of local families included children under 15. That is well above the Greater Brisbane average.
These are suburb-wide household figures, not school or childcare ratings.
What is the age profile of Warner?The balance between younger adults, families and older residents changes how a suburb feels day to day.+
Warner had 12,264 residents at the 2021 Census.
31.9% of residents were aged 25 to 44.
18.6% were 55 or older. That is well below the Greater Brisbane average.
Age shares use the suburb’s total Census population.
What kind of homes are in Warner?The dwelling mix shows whether the suburb is mostly detached homes, townhouses or apartments.+
Separate houses made up 91.3% of occupied private dwellings in Warner.
Townhouses and other attached homes represented 8.6% of occupied private dwellings.
Flats and apartments accounted for 0.3% of occupied private dwellings.
All three shares use occupied private dwellings as the denominator.
Do people own or rent in Warner?Tenure shows how many households own outright, have a mortgage or rent.+
20.3% of households with a stated tenure owned their home outright. That is well below the Greater Brisbane average.
49.1% were buying their home with a mortgage.
30.6% rented their home. That is somewhat below the Greater Brisbane average.
Tenure shares include households that stated one of these three arrangements.
How large are households in Warner?Household-size data shows the mix behind the suburb-wide average.+
The average household in Warner had 3.0 people.
One-person households made up 13.1% of occupied homes. That is well below the Greater Brisbane average.
Two-person households represented 30.4%, while 36.2% had four or more usual residents.
Household size counts usual residents in occupied homes.
How do people get to work in Warner?Census-day travel shows the balance between home, road, public transport and active travel.+
16.3% of workers worked from home on Census day. That is close to the Greater Brisbane average.
62.2% drove a car as their only travel method. That is well above the Greater Brisbane average.
2.1% used only a train, tram or bus, while 0.7% walked or cycled.
Travel figures are a 2021 Census-day snapshot.
How many cars do households have in Warner?Vehicle availability helps describe the routines local households can support.+
1.2% of occupied households had no motor vehicle available. That is about half the Greater Brisbane rate.
One-car households formed 23.8% of the total.
75.0% of households had two or more vehicles.
Vehicle counts refer to occupied households, not individual residents.
Who's moving to Warner, and who stays?Address history shows how settled or recently mobile residents were at the Census date.+
53.4% were at the same address five years earlier, while 46.6% reported a different address.
21.5% of residents were born outside Australia. That is somewhat below the Greater Brisbane average.
Mobility figures cover residents who answered the relevant Census question.
What languages are spoken in Warner?Language at home gives a direct view of the community’s everyday linguistic mix.+
86.8% of residents used only English at home.
11.0% used a language other than English at home.
0.5% spoke Mandarin at home, 0.2% spoke Cantonese and 1.3% spoke Hindi.
Home language is separate from English proficiency.
Where were Warner residents born?Birthplace adds another layer to the local story, separate from ancestry and language.+
21.5% of Warner's population was born outside Australia, while roughly 78.5% was born in Australia.
0.3% of residents were born in China.
1.7% were born in India, while Australian citizenship across the whole suburb stood at 91.0%.
Birthplace, citizenship and ancestry describe different parts of identity.
What ancestries shape Warner?Ancestry responses show how residents describe family and cultural background.+
English ancestry accounted for 40.3% of ancestry responses in Warner.
Australian ancestry made up 40.8% of responses.
Chinese ancestry represented 1.4% of responses.
People may nominate more than one ancestry, so these are shares of responses.
What work do people do in Warner?Occupation describes a person’s role; industry describes the sector employing them.+
20.3% of employed residents worked in professional occupations. That is somewhat below the Greater Brisbane average.
Managers accounted for 12.9% of employed residents, while clerical and administrative workers made up 17.5%.
Health care and social assistance employed 16.0% of workers, and education and training employed 7.7%.
Occupation and industry figures describe employed residents of the suburb.
How active is the workforce in Warner?Participation, employment pattern and unemployment describe the resident labour force.+
The labour-force participation rate was 75.5%, compared with 64.5% across Greater Brisbane.
45.9% of residents aged 15 and over worked full time, while 20.7% worked part time.
Unemployment was 3.7%, compared with 5.5% across Greater Brisbane.
Labour-force figures come from the 2021 Census.
What does the health profile of Warner show?Selected long-term health conditions help describe community service needs.+
63.5% reported no selected long-term health condition.
10.1% reported a long-term mental health condition, including depression or anxiety.
9.2% reported asthma, 6.0% arthritis and 3.5% diabetes excluding gestational diabetes.
These are self-reported suburb-wide Census figures, not address-level health risks.
How educated is Warner?Qualifications describe the study pathways represented among local residents.+
25.7% of people in the qualifications table held a bachelor degree. That is close to the Greater Brisbane average.
Postgraduate degrees accounted for 5.6% of qualification responses.
Diploma-level qualifications represented 18.8%, while certificate-level qualifications represented 40.3%.
Qualification categories are reported separately and are not school ratings.
What do incomes and housing costs look like in Warner?Census income and household payments give historical context for local living costs.+

The median monthly mortgage repayment reported by local households was $1,980.
Occupied renting households reported a median rent of $420 per week.

How does Warner compare with Greater Brisbane?A few clear differences place the suburb in its wider metropolitan context.+
