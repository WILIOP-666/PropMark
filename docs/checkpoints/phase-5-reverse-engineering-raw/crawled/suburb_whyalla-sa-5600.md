## Questions people ask about Whyalla
Open any question for a plain-English reading of the public 2021 Census for Whyalla SA 5600.
Is Whyalla family-friendly?Age, family structure and household size describe the community families would be joining.+
15.5% of Whyalla's residents were children under 15 in the 2021 Census. That is close to the Rest of SA average.
959 family households lived in Whyalla; 38.5% were couple families and 12.5% were one-parent families.
32.3% of local families included children under 15. That is almost exactly typical for Rest of SA.
These are suburb-wide household figures, not school or childcare ratings.
What is the age profile of Whyalla?The balance between younger adults, families and older residents changes how a suburb feels day to day.+
Whyalla had 3,609 residents at the 2021 Census.
21.8% of residents were aged 25 to 44.
36.3% were 55 or older. That is somewhat below the Rest of SA average.
Age shares use the suburb’s total Census population.
What kind of homes are in Whyalla?The dwelling mix shows whether the suburb is mostly detached homes, townhouses or apartments.+
Separate houses made up 75.6% of occupied private dwellings in Whyalla.
Townhouses and other attached homes represented 17.5% of occupied private dwellings.
Flats and apartments accounted for 6.2% of occupied private dwellings.
All three shares use occupied private dwellings as the denominator.
Do people own or rent in Whyalla?Tenure shows how many households own outright, have a mortgage or rent.+
33.9% of households with a stated tenure owned their home outright. That is well below the Rest of SA average.
36.8% were buying their home with a mortgage.
29.3% rented their home. That is somewhat above the Rest of SA average.
Tenure shares include households that stated one of these three arrangements.
How large are households in Whyalla?Household-size data shows the mix behind the suburb-wide average.+
The average household in Whyalla had 2.2 people.
One-person households made up 33.6% of occupied homes. That is close to the Rest of SA average.
Two-person households represented 36.6%, while 17.7% had four or more usual residents.
Household size counts usual residents in occupied homes.
How do people get to work in Whyalla?Census-day travel shows the balance between home, road, public transport and active travel.+
2.6% of workers worked from home on Census day. That is about half the Rest of SA rate.
67.5% drove a car as their only travel method. That is close to the Rest of SA average.
1.6% used only a train, tram or bus, while 5.8% walked or cycled.
Travel figures are a 2021 Census-day snapshot.
How many cars do households have in Whyalla?Vehicle availability helps describe the routines local households can support.+
7.0% of occupied households had no motor vehicle available. That is close to the Rest of SA average.
One-car households formed 35.3% of the total.
57.6% of households had two or more vehicles.
Vehicle counts refer to occupied households, not individual residents.
Who's moving to Whyalla, and who stays?Address history shows how settled or recently mobile residents were at the Census date.+
66.8% were at the same address five years earlier, while 33.2% reported a different address.
19.8% of residents were born outside Australia. That is well above the Rest of SA average.
Mobility figures cover residents who answered the relevant Census question.
What languages are spoken in Whyalla?Language at home gives a direct view of the community’s everyday linguistic mix.+
88.5% of residents used only English at home.
7.0% used a language other than English at home.
0.2% spoke Mandarin at home, 0.0% spoke Cantonese and 0.4% spoke Hindi.
Home language is separate from English proficiency.
Where were Whyalla residents born?Birthplace adds another layer to the local story, separate from ancestry and language.+
19.8% of Whyalla's population was born outside Australia, while roughly 80.2% was born in Australia.
0.1% of residents were born in China.
1.4% were born in India, while Australian citizenship across the whole suburb stood at 88.7%.
Birthplace, citizenship and ancestry describe different parts of identity.
What ancestries shape Whyalla?Ancestry responses show how residents describe family and cultural background.+
English ancestry accounted for 41.3% of ancestry responses in Whyalla.
Australian ancestry made up 37.5% of responses.
Chinese ancestry represented 0.5% of responses.
People may nominate more than one ancestry, so these are shares of responses.
What work do people do in Whyalla?Occupation describes a person’s role; industry describes the sector employing them.+
20.7% of employed residents worked in professional occupations. That is well above the Rest of SA average.
Managers accounted for 11.9% of employed residents, while clerical and administrative workers made up 10.1%.
Health care and social assistance employed 17.1% of workers, and education and training employed 10.4%.
Occupation and industry figures describe employed residents of the suburb.
How active is the workforce in Whyalla?Participation, employment pattern and unemployment describe the resident labour force.+
The labour-force participation rate was 60.9%, compared with 54.1% across Rest of SA.
37.2% of residents aged 15 and over worked full time, while 16.9% worked part time.
Unemployment was 5.2%, compared with 4.8% across Rest of SA.
Labour-force figures come from the 2021 Census.
What does the health profile of Whyalla show?Selected long-term health conditions help describe community service needs.+
56.6% reported no selected long-term health condition.
8.4% reported a long-term mental health condition, including depression or anxiety.
9.6% reported asthma, 12.1% arthritis and 6.7% diabetes excluding gestational diabetes.
These are self-reported suburb-wide Census figures, not address-level health risks.
How educated is Whyalla?Qualifications describe the study pathways represented among local residents.+
19.6% of people in the qualifications table held a bachelor degree. That is somewhat above the Rest of SA average.
Postgraduate degrees accounted for 4.7% of qualification responses.
Diploma-level qualifications represented 12.8%, while certificate-level qualifications represented 46.3%.
Qualification categories are reported separately and are not school ratings.
What do incomes and housing costs look like in Whyalla?Census income and household payments give historical context for local living costs.+
Median household income was $1,658 per week in the 2021 Census.
The median monthly mortgage repayment reported by local households was $1,300.
Occupied renting households reported a median rent of $200 per week.
These household figures are from 2021; current sale and asking-rent medians appear in the market section.
How does Whyalla compare with Rest of SA?A few clear differences place the suburb in its wider metropolitan context.+

Renting accounted for 29.3% in Whyalla, against 26.0% across Rest of SA.

## A buyer’s quick guide to Whyalla
### Is Whyalla expensive?
The median house price is $446,000, while units and apartments sit at $214,000. Bedroom count, land and condition still create a wide range around those suburb medians.
### Is Whyalla good for families?
The Census counted 959 local families, and 15.5% of residents were under 15. That describes the community; school quality, travel times and the right fit for a particular family need separate research.
### What can I get for $1.5m in Whyalla?
Larger houses have a median around $620,000, the closest bedroom benchmark we hold to a $1.5m budget. Individual homes can differ substantially with block size, renovation and location.
